Your Church Survey · November 2026

Our Strength

Inspiring worship service

— “This is a place we believe is worth coming to—and worth bringing others to.”

Many of us come ready to participate, and we can see something in our worship gatherings that matters. There is a sense that what happens here can connect with real life and help us turn toward God. Perhaps most encouragingly, we believe these gatherings can also be a place where people who are not yet part of church life might come and find something meaningful.

That does not mean every gathering reaches every one of us in the same way. Some of us do not always look forward to coming, and there are times when worship can leave us less engaged than we would hope. But underneath that tension is something we value: gathering together still has purpose for us, and we want to be part of it rather than simply watch from the edges.

Thank you for giving us a shared place where we can come, participate, encounter God and welcome others into something we ourselves value.

Our Struggle

Effective structures

— “We want to contribute, but we don’t always know how all our efforts fit together.”

There is willingness and ability among us. Many of us know something of what God has given us, can see value in what we contribute, and experience God using it. Yet it can be difficult to see where we are going together or how the different parts of our church connect. Planning, coordination and shared responsibility do not always give us the clarity and support we need to offer what we have with confidence.

That can leave a gap between wanting to contribute and experiencing ourselves as part of a genuinely shared endeavour. We do not simply want more things to do. We want to know that what each of us brings belongs within something we understand and can participate in together.

Please help us experience church life as something we share—not just through the tasks we each perform, but through clearer purpose, stronger connection and the sense that we really are carrying it together.

What draws us near

Inspiring worship service

— “Maybe I could come and see for myself.”

If we were wondering whether to come closer, it would matter that this appears to be a church where people beyond the existing community already find their way into worship. We might hope to encounter something more than a gathering designed only for those who already know how everything works: something connected with real life, where people come prepared to participate and where encountering God is genuinely possible.

We might not expect every part of the experience to connect immediately. But knowing that people already inside can readily say why they come—and that there seems to be an openness to people who are not yet part of the church—could make crossing the threshold feel less daunting. Thank you for making it imaginable that there could be something here for us too.

What gives us pause

Effective structures

— “If I came closer, would I eventually find where I fit?”

Getting through the door is one thing; finding a meaningful place within a community is another. If we stayed, or came back and wanted to become involved again, we might wonder how easily we could discover what we have to offer, understand where the church is going, and find our place within what everyone is doing together.

There are encouraging signs that people who are already contributing can experience God using what they bring. That makes the question more important, not less: would there be a clear way for someone moving in from the edges to grow from being welcomed into genuinely belonging and participating? Please make room for us not only to arrive, but eventually to discover how we might belong and contribute too.

What you can take heart in

“There is already life here that you do not have to manufacture.”

You are carrying responsibility among people who already bring significant life with them. There are people here who recognise their gifts, believe God uses what they contribute, deepen relationships beyond the church and participate actively when the church gathers. There is also a strong practical instinct to help people in need. This is not a body waiting passively for a few people to make church happen for them.

That matters for you. You do not have to be the source of all the energy, faithfulness or capacity the church needs. Indeed, part of what is already present can be received by you: other people have something from God to bring to this body. Their contribution can become part of what sustains you as well as what extends the church’s life.

Take heart in that. Your responsibility is exercised among people in whom God is already at work.

What needs to be carried

“Help the life that is already present become genuinely shared life.”

The more searching question is not whether people are willing to contribute, but whether the church is sufficiently able to hold those contributions together. People can often recognise what they bring and see value in it, yet the shared picture is much less clear: where the church is going, how its different parts fit together, how ministry is planned, how people are equipped, and how their contribution is reviewed and supported.

There is a particular stewardship issue here because collaboration itself appears fragile. The experience that leaders can prefer doing the work themselves rather than working with others sits alongside indications that leaders do not always remain concentrated on the work for which they are gifted. The survey cannot tell you why that pattern exists, nor does it establish that those carrying responsibility caused the wider constraint. But it does make the question difficult to avoid: is responsibility being carried in ways that help the capacity already present throughout the body become shared capacity?

That may require you to carry some things very deliberately. Clarity of purpose, genuine collaboration, coordination and the development of others do not simply emerge because willing people are present. Yet carrying responsibility for those conditions is different from carrying all the ministry yourself. Some things are yours to hold precisely so that other things no longer need to be.

There is freedom as well as challenge in that distinction. You can cultivate conditions in which people understand where they are going together, receive support for what they carry and increasingly contribute alongside one another. You cannot manufacture the life that results. You can plant and water faithfully, make room for the gifts God has already distributed through the body, and allow yourself to receive from them too. God gives the growth.
